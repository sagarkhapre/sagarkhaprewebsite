Nagpur & Vidarbha

Is Nagpur still the green city, or just green on paper?

45°C+ summers. A 2023 flood. And a High Court asking: "Where is the greenery?"

By Sagar Khapre · 28 November 2026 · 5 min read

We grew up calling Nagpur a green city: tiger capital, orange city, wide tree-lined roads. In 2026 the story looks different.

Summers crossed 45°C. Old trees are being cut for roads, sewage lines and buildings. And in June, the Bombay High Court's Nagpur bench asked a simple question about one of our busiest roads: where is the greenery?

This Sunday I'm attending Green Nagpur's Influencers Meet. Before I go, here's what's going wrong, and what I'll be asking about.

My takeSagar Khapre · Host, Sagar Khapre Are Baapre

Planting a sapling 15 km away doesn't give my street its shade back.

I've spent 15 years in IT. In software, if you delete something important, you don't "compensate" by adding something random somewhere else. You fix the thing you broke. Trees work the same way. The tree outside your house is the one that cools your house.

I'm not against development. Nagpur needs metro, roads and drainage. But development that makes the city hotter and floods it faster isn't development. It's a bill our children will pay.

On my podcast, I go to my guests' ground and check things myself. So this Sunday I'm going to listen, ask hard questions, and come back with solutions you can actually use.

— Sagar Khapre

Problem 1: Trees on paper, not on streets

The biggest fight right now is London Street. In June 2026, the High Court bench of Justices Anil Kilor and Raj Wakode questioned how trees were cut for the project and how "compensatory" planting was done.

Figures from NMC's affidavit, as reported by The Live Nagpur and The Hitavada.

The court's point was simple: planting trees somewhere else doesn't restore the area where mature trees were cut. It ordered a joint inspection and said 1,000 more trees must be planted in the locality if space is found. In August, residents hugged trees in a Chipko-style protest on the same road.

It's not just one road:

  • Aamrai, near the airport: NMC proposed cutting about 60 trees for a sewage line. Some are up to 93 years old, and three are heritage trees.
  • Ajni Vann: a few years ago, an intermodal station plan raised fears that up to 40,000 trees could go in phases. Citizens fought back, and the tender was scrapped in 2022. The area is still being watched.

Problem 2: A city that's heating up

In May 2026, Nagpur recorded its hottest day of the season at 45.5°C, with nights close to 30°C across Vidarbha. Fewer trees and more concrete mean the city soaks up heat all day and gives it back all night.

A NEERI study quoted by The Hitavada puts Nagpur's tree canopy at about 19.5%, while state tree law asks for 33% green cover at ward level.

"The tree outside your house is the one that cools your house."— Sagar Khapre

Problem 3: When rain has nowhere to go

On 23 September 2023, about 109 mm of heavy rain flooded large parts of the city. Four people died and around 10,000 houses were affected. In July 2025, Ambazari and Gorewada lakes came close to overflowing again after three days of rain.

The state has now sanctioned ₹70 crore for Ambazari dam safety and Nag river work, including widening and deepening about 5 km of the river. That's needed. But when the whole city is concrete, there's less soil left to soak up the rain.

Problem 4: Water stress

Hotter summers mean more water use. Fewer trees and paved ground mean less rainwater going back into the soil. Flooding in the monsoon and shortage in the summer are two sides of the same problem.

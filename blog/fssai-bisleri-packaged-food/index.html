After the Bisleri batch and the Supreme Court order, will you still trust packaged food?

1 water batch off the shelves. 3 red warnings coming to your snacks.

By Sagar Khapre · 27 November 2026 · 5 min read
Read the packet
Supreme Court order · 26 Nov 2026
3red warnings coming

In one week, two food stories hit every Indian kitchen. The Supreme Court wants red warnings on unhealthy packaged food. And FSSAI stopped the sale of one batch of Bisleri water.

Biscuits, namkeen, juice, bottled water. We buy them every day without a second look. So the real question is simple: should you still trust what comes in a packet? Here's what actually happened, what it means for you, and my take.

My takeSagar Khapre · Host, Sagar Khapre Are Baapre

Don't panic. Read the packet.

One batch of water failing a test doesn't mean every bottle in India is unsafe. But this week told me something about all of us: we trust a brand name more than we read a label.

When I sat with a cardiologist for my episode on young heart attacks, he told me the youngest heart attack patient he has seen was just 23, and processed food is one of the big reasons. I said it on that episode too: when I was a kid, packaged food was not this common. Today even a small kirana shop in a village has packets hanging everywhere.

I've spent 15 years in IT. We never ship code without testing it. On my podcast, I check a guest's claim myself before I believe it. Do the same with your food: turn the packet over, read for 10 seconds, then decide.

The red label is a good start. But a label works only if we read it. And good companies don't need to wait a full year. They can start tomorrow.

— Sagar Khapre

What the Supreme Court ordered

On 26 November, a bench of Justices J B Pardiwala and K Vinod Chandran told FSSAI to bring in clear front-of-pack warnings on packaged foods that are high in fat, sugar or salt. The case was a public interest petition by the group 3S and Our Health Society.

Illustration of the kind of warning the court described: a separate red hexagon for each nutrient.

The important part: FSSAI had suggested one symbol for all three. The court said no. Each nutrient gets its own red hexagon, so you can see at a glance if your snack is high in sugar, salt, fat, or all three. Reports say the warning should cover around 4 to 5% of the front of the pack, and the court wants total sugar and saturated fat counted, not just "added" sugar.

The Bisleri batch: what we actually know

On 22 November, FSSAI said it had approved prosecution against Bisleri after a water sample failed lab tests. A day later, FSSAI clarified that the stop-sale order covers only one batch, GO 270, made at the company's Goa plant in September 2025. Here are both sides, as reported:

What FSSAI says

  • One sample failed for bromate, and a later test flagged PAHs (a type of chemical contaminant).
  • Only one batch was found non-compliant. Its sale is prohibited.
  • The referral lab's report is final under the law.

What Bisleri says

  • No stock of that batch is in the market now.
  • It disputes the test results and had asked for a retest.
  • All 143 of its plants are operating and compliant.

FSSAI has also started wider checks on packaged drinking water across the country. Its chief, Rajit Punhani, said the drive is meant to make companies comply, not to spread panic, and asked why the industry should fail even one sample.

"We trust a brand name more than we read a label. That has to change."— Sagar Khapre

What you can check today

  1. Sugar, salt and fat per 100 g. It's already on the back of the pack. Compare two brands before you buy.
  2. The FSSAI licence number. Every legit packaged food carries one. No number, no purchase.
  3. Batch number and date. On water bottles too. If a batch is ever flagged, this is how you know.
  4. The serving size trick. A "small" number per serving can hide a big number per packet.
  5. Health claims on the front. "Multigrain" or "baked" doesn't mean low sugar or low salt. Read the back.

Will a red label really change anything?

Warning labels work best when they are simple and hard to miss, which is exactly why the court insisted on separate symbols. But habits change slowly. The label will help those who look. The rest of us still have to look.

My honest view: this is not a "packaged food is poison" moment. It's a "know what you're eating" moment. Eat the biscuit if you want it. Just know what's in it.
